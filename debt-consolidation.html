How to compare a debt consolidation option

Start by listing each existing loan or card balance, its monthly payment, interest rate, remaining tenure and any amount or charge required to close it. This gives you a fair baseline before comparing a new repayment.

  1. Compare total cost, not only the new EMI. Include the new interest rate, processing fee, other charges, tenure and total repayment. A lower EMI over a longer term can cost more overall.
  2. Check the terms in writing. Ask for the full repayment schedule and all applicable charges before accepting an offer. Confirm which existing dues will be paid and how closure will be confirmed.
  3. Keep paying current dues. Continue existing EMIs and card payments until the relevant lender confirms settlement. An enquiry or provisional offer does not close an account.

Consolidation may not be suitable if the new loan costs more overall, if existing balances are almost repaid, or if the new EMI still does not fit your budget. Do not assume a new loan automatically improves your CIBIL score; credit enquiries can appear in your report. Read TransUnion CIBIL’s guide to the Accounts and Enquiries sections.

Debt consolidation FAQs

What is a debt consolidation loan?

It is a new loan or credit facility used to repay selected existing debts, so you make repayments under the new lender’s terms. It does not write off what you owe. Confirm which old balances will be repaid and how each account’s closure will be recorded.

Can multiple personal loans and credit-card dues be combined?

Some lenders may consider eligible personal-loan balances and credit-card dues together. The account types, amounts and payout process accepted vary by lender and product. Ask for written confirmation of which balances are covered before proceeding.

Will one EMI always reduce my total cost?

No. A lower monthly instalment can come from extending the repayment tenure, which may increase the total interest and amount repaid. Compare the full repayment cost, not only the new EMI.

What should I compare before accepting an offer?

Review the interest rate and APR where applicable, processing and other charges, tenure, repayment schedule, total amount payable, and any prepayment or foreclosure conditions. Also confirm how the selected old dues will be paid and closed.

Will applying for debt consolidation affect my CIBIL score?

A lender’s credit check made during a loan application may appear in the Enquiries section of your credit report. TransUnion CIBIL explains that applications to several lenders within a short period can create multiple enquiries and may affect your score. Ask which lender will access your report and when; do not assume every enquiry is a soft check.

Should I stop paying my existing EMIs after submitting an enquiry?

No. Keep paying your existing loan EMIs and card dues on time until the relevant lender confirms that the selected balance has been paid and the account has been updated or closed. Submitting an enquiry or receiving a provisional offer does not close an account.

Does a LoansBharti enquiry guarantee loan approval?

No. LoansBharti can review your enquiry and help you understand options that may be worth exploring. The relevant lender independently decides eligibility, approval, interest rate, charges, tenure and disbursal.

For a covered new retail term loan, read the lender’s Key Facts Statement (KFS) before signing. RBI’s 15 April 2024 directions define APR as the annual cost of credit including interest and associated charges, and require the KFS to include an APR computation and repayment schedule. The circular excludes credit-card receivables from these KFS provisions, so check your card issuer’s own terms separately. Read the RBI KFS directions.

TransUnion CIBIL explains how lender enquiries appear in a credit report and how repeated applications over a short period may affect a score. Read CIBIL’s guide to Accounts and Enquiries.
