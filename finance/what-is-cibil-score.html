What Is a CIBIL Score? Range, Report & Ways to Check It

Learn what a CIBIL score represents, how it differs from a credit report, what may influence it and how to review errors.

LoansBharti Editorial Desk · 1 November 2026 · 6 min read

A CIBIL score is a three-digit summary of an individual's credit history generated by TransUnion CIBIL using information in the person's credit report. Lenders may use it, along with income, existing obligations, employment and their own policies, while assessing a credit application.

Important: A score is not a loan approval guarantee. Each lender sets its own eligibility rules and may consider more than the score.

What is the score range?

TransUnion CIBIL commonly provides a score in the range of 300 to 900 for individuals. A higher score is generally viewed more favourably by lenders, but there is no universal score that guarantees approval or a particular interest rate.

What can influence a credit score?

  • Payment history: whether instalments and card payments are made on time.
  • Credit utilisation: how much of available revolving credit is being used.
  • Length of credit history: how long credit accounts have been active.
  • Credit mix: the types of credit accounts in the report.
  • Recent applications: multiple applications and enquiries may be considered in scoring.

Scoring models are proprietary, so the exact weight of each factor should not be assumed.

Score vs credit report: what is the difference?

The score is a number. The credit report is the underlying record of accounts, balances, payment history, enquiries and personal details. Reviewing the report helps you understand the information behind the score and spot possible errors.

How can you check your report?

  1. Use the official website of the credit information company.
  2. Complete its identity verification process.
  3. Download or view the report and check personal details and every listed account.
  4. Dispute any incorrect account, balance or payment-history entry through the bureau and lender.

India has four RBI-licensed credit information companies. You can consult the official bureau websites for their current report access and dispute processes.

Healthy credit habits

  • Pay bills and EMIs by the due date.
  • Keep credit-card utilisation manageable.
  • Avoid applying for several loans at once without comparing options.
  • Review your report periodically and correct genuine errors.
  • Do not close old accounts without understanding the impact on your overall credit profile.

Official information

Check your report directly with TransUnion CIBIL and review applicable rules on the RBI website. General educational content only; lender policies and scoring models can change.
