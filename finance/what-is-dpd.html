What Is DPD in a CIBIL Report? Meaning, Codes & Impact

DPD means Days Past Due. Learn how repayment delays may appear in a credit report, how to check the entry and what to do if it looks incorrect.

LoansBharti Editorial Desk · 1 November 2026 · 6 min read

DPD stands for Days Past Due. In a credit report, it generally indicates how many days a payment was overdue for a particular credit account during a reported period. It helps lenders understand repayment behaviour over time.

In simple words: DPD is a record of whether a loan or credit-card payment was made on time or after its due date. The exact display and reporting period depend on the credit information company and the data supplied by the lender.

How is DPD shown?

Credit reports may show a month-wise repayment history using numbers or status codes. A numeric value can indicate the number of days a payment was overdue for the relevant reporting period. A value such as 000 or STD may indicate that the account was reported as standard/current for that period, depending on the report format.

Do not assume every code has the same meaning across every report. Read the legend or help section supplied by the credit bureau that issued your report.

Why can DPD matter?

  • It gives a lender information about past repayment patterns.
  • Repeated or recent delays may affect how a lender assesses an application.
  • It can help you identify a missed payment or a reporting error.

A credit score is calculated using multiple factors. DPD is one part of the repayment information and does not, by itself, tell you whether a new loan will be approved.

What should you do if DPD looks incorrect?

  1. Download your latest credit report from the relevant credit bureau.
  2. Check the account, month, overdue amount and payment date against your bank or lender records.
  3. Raise a dispute with the credit bureau and contact the lender that supplied the information.
  4. Keep the complaint reference and supporting payment proof until the issue is resolved.

Do not pay an unknown agent who promises to erase accurate repayment history. Corrections must be based on verified information from the lender and bureau process.

How can you avoid future DPD entries?

Set reminders or auto-pay where suitable, keep enough balance before the due date and review statements regularly. If you anticipate difficulty paying, contact the lender before the due date to understand available options. Any restructuring or settlement can have consequences—ask for the terms in writing.

Sources and further reading

For the rules and your specific report, consult the credit bureau that issued it and your lender. RBI's official website publishes directions and FAQs relevant to credit information reporting: rbi.org.in.
