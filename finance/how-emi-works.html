How Does EMI Work? Principal, Interest & Loan Tenure Explained

Understand how a loan EMI is calculated, why interest and tenure matter, and what to compare before choosing a repayment plan.

LoansBharti Editorial Desk · 1 November 2026 · 5 min read

EMI means Equated Monthly Instalment. It is the scheduled amount a borrower pays each month to repay a loan over an agreed tenure. For a standard reducing-balance loan, each EMI includes a principal component and an interest component.

Remember: A lower EMI does not always mean a cheaper loan. A longer tenure can reduce the monthly instalment while increasing the total interest paid.

What affects your EMI?

  • Principal: the amount borrowed.
  • Interest rate: the rate charged by the lender and how it is applied.
  • Tenure: the time allowed to repay the loan.
  • Fees and charges: processing fees and other charges can increase the overall borrowing cost even if they are not part of the EMI.

How is EMI calculated?

For a standard monthly reducing-balance loan, the commonly used formula is:

EMI = P × r × (1 + r)n ÷ ((1 + r)n − 1)
Here P is the principal, r is the monthly interest rate (annual rate divided by 12), and n is the number of monthly instalments.

This formula is an illustration for a typical fixed-rate reducing-balance loan. Actual lender calculations can vary by product, rate type, payment dates and terms.

Why does tenure matter?

Suppose two loans have the same principal and interest rate, but one is repaid over a longer period. The longer-tenure loan will generally have a lower monthly EMI, but interest accrues for more months. Compare the total repayment, not just the monthly amount.

What should you compare before accepting an offer?

  • Annual interest rate and whether it is fixed or floating.
  • Total amount payable over the full tenure.
  • Processing fees, taxes and other charges.
  • Prepayment or foreclosure conditions.
  • Whether the EMI fits your budget after essential expenses.
