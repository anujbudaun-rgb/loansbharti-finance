Debt Consolidation – FAQs

Understand how loan consolidation works before you decide.

What is loan consolidation?

Loan consolidation means combining multiple eligible debts—such as personal-loan balances or credit-card dues—into one new repayment facility, where available. Instead of managing several payments, you may have one EMI to manage.

How does debt consolidation work?

First, your existing loan balances, monthly EMIs, income and repayment history are reviewed. If a suitable option is available, a new facility may be used to repay selected existing dues. You then repay the new facility according to its agreed EMI, interest rate, fees and tenure. Always compare the total repayment cost before proceeding.

Does debt consolidation affect my CIBIL score?

It can affect your CIBIL score. A lender's credit enquiry may be recorded when you apply. Opening a new loan and closing old accounts can also affect your credit profile. Paying the new EMI on time may support your repayment history over time, while missed or late payments can hurt your score. The exact impact varies by profile and lender reporting.

Will my CIBIL score increase after loan consolidation?

Not automatically. Your score depends on several factors, including payment history, credit utilisation, outstanding balances, account age and recent enquiries. Consolidation may help you manage repayments, but no score increase can be guaranteed.

Can I consolidate personal loans and credit-card dues together?

Depending on the available product and your profile, eligible personal-loan balances and credit-card dues may be considered. The types and amounts accepted vary by provider.

Will debt consolidation reduce my monthly EMI?

It may reduce your monthly outgo if the new repayment amount is lower, but a longer tenure can increase the total interest paid. Compare the EMI, interest rate, processing fees, foreclosure charges and total repayment amount—not just the monthly instalment.

Who can enquire for debt consolidation?

Salaried applicants with existing personal-loan EMIs or credit-card dues can share their details for an initial review. Income, existing obligations, credit profile, location and provider criteria may affect the options available.

What documents may be needed?

Depending on the provider and case, you may be asked for identity and address proof, salary slips, bank statements, and current loan or credit-card statements. Share documents only through verified, secure channels.

Does applying for consolidation close my existing loans automatically?

No. Do not stop paying existing EMIs or card dues just because you have submitted an enquiry. Continue payments until the relevant lender confirms that the dues have been settled and the accounts updated.
